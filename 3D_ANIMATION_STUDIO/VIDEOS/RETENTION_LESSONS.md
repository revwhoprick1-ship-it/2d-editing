## What made them leave (and the evidence)

1. **Frame 0 without a character in danger.** Benched P1 opened on a scoreboard graphic: 42.6% stayed, the worst.
   Vase and Bus opened on a character with a threat already moving: 76% and 74%. Same channel, same style, about the same length.
2. **Going back in time right after the hook.** Benched P1 cuts to "two months ago" at 7.8 s, and its curve falls hardest
   from 0 to 12 s (down to about 65%). Kessrah does "12 hours earlier" at 3.4 s and is the weakest reel (12.8k).
   The hook makes a promise, and the flashback breaks it right away.
3. **Recaps.** Benched P2 holds flat for the first 4 s (the mid-air shot hook works), then drops sharply from 4 to 8 s.
   The recap starts at 4.8 s. Even the 5 s recap in Vase P2 came with an 81% fall from Part 1.
4. **"Part 2" itself.** Both Part 2s lost 62–81% of the Part 1 reach on IG. With about 1% followers, viewers have no context
   and the title tells them they're missing something.
5. **The end choice card.** It fills the last 8–11 s (about 15%) of every video, and every curve's steepest drop sits
   inside that window. The story is over, so people swipe.
6. **Genre and length.** On YouTube, horror got 8–10k views and basketball about 1k. The longest video (Kessrah,
   88 s) got the fewest views. The best videos are 58–67 s.

What worked and should stay: rewatches (the curve starts at 130–150%) from loops, a hook with a rule plus an instant
consequence (Vase: "one rule..." then "you just broke it"), a new question every few seconds, shareable fear (Vase: 765 shares).

## Rules from now on
- Frame 0 = a character + a threat already moving + a sound hit. Never a graphic, title card or empty set.
- The story moves forward only: no "X hours earlier", no "previously", no recap. Context goes into dialogue lines.
- Make standalone stories, each complete with its own twist. Never put "Part 2" in the title.
- No choice card at the end. The twist plays in the last 3 s and loops into frame 0. Comments are asked for in the pinned comment.
- Length 60–75 s. First scare by 15 s, the big event by about 50% of the video, a reveal or chase after it, the twist at the end.
- A new picture every 2 s or less, a new question every 5 s or less.

Rules added:
- **Outro/CTA under 3 s** ("1 open, 2 stay, 3 run. Comment now."). The card can stay on screen, but the narration must end fast and loop.
- **Never reset tension**: no "...and then everything was normal again". Every scene raises the stakes from the last one.
- **~58 s beats 67 s**: a shorter runtime leaves more viewers on screen at the loop point (45.6 % vs 30.9 %).
- **Movement**: characters must MOVE (run, vault, slide, crouch, turn, breathe, shake), not stand still in frame. The camera tracks the action.
