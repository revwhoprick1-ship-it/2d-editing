# VIDEO_002: What VIDEO_001 taught us (60.7% viewed vs. swiped)

"Viewed vs swiped away" is decided in roughly the **first 1–2 seconds**, before the story can do any work. I re‑inspected VIDEO_001's opening frame by frame. Here is what a scroller actually got:

| Time | What the viewer saw / heard | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| frame 0 | The **top of the mascot's slumped head** (brown hair filling the frame) | No subject, no premise, no face. The frame could be anything. |
| 0.0–0.33 s | Still hair. Narration starts "You've been shrunk…" | The face only appears at 0.33 s. |
| 0.3–2.3 s | Slow crane‑out reveal | A slow move during the most valuable seconds |
| 0.2–4.6 s | One long hook sentence (4.4 s) | The premise and threat land only at ~4 s |
| whole video | Average shot ≈ 2.85 s, 20 shots, captions small (78 px), no on‑screen graphics | Pacing calmer than modern Shorts; little visual punctuation |
| ending | A joke ending on a separate shot | Not a loop; nothing invites a rewatch |

## What changes in VIDEO_002 (and why)
1. **Frame 0 = the event itself.** A packed elevator at the exact instant everyone jolts, with gas puffs, big title text and a chorus SFX on the first audio sample. The premise is readable with **sound off** (text + visual) and **sound on** (the sound *is* the joke).
2. **Premise in under 2.5 s.** First line ≤ 10 words, starting at 0.05 s.
3. **Editing density.** Target average shot ≈ 1.2–1.8 s, with deliberate variation: fast during chaos, a held beat before punchlines, a silence beat. Use punch‑ins, whip pans, split screens, freeze frames, flashes, speed ramps, a CCTV "ENHANCE" sequence, a VHS rewind and motion graphics, each motivated by a story beat.
4. **Bigger, livelier captions.** Word‑by‑word highlight, 92 px, emphasis colour, pop animation. Captions stay out of faces.
5. **Loop by design.** The last beat is the *cause* of the first beat (circular story). The same camera, the same set, and continuous audio across the cut.
6. **Mascot in almost every beat** (reaction inserts during montages), so the viewer always has a face to follow.
7. **Shorter.** 30–40 s, with every line pulling weight.
8. **Faster renders.** Simpler sets that only build what the camera sees, extras without subdivision, in‑camera punch‑ins (no resolution loss), stills for freeze/CCTV/zoom effects, previews at 270p, re‑render only changed frames.
