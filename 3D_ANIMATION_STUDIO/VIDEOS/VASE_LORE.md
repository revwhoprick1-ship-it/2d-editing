# "Don't Touch The Vase" — series lore bible (keep every part consistent with this)

## The house
* The family lives in **Grandma's old house** (Part 1 HUD: "GRANDMA'S HOUSE"). Grandma (**Rose**) grew up there and moved out
  decades ago; she now lives alone **three streets away** (Part 2). She left the vase on the shelf and gave one rule: never touch it.

## 1968 — what happened
* Rose was 12 and had a **twin brother, Eli**. Something began living in the walls of the house. It copied voices. Eli stopped blinking.
* Their mother was a **porcelain painter**. She painted **every room of the house onto a vase** (the blue pattern = a map of the house),
  so the thing would believe the vase *was* the house. It can only live inside a home.
* They lured it in by calling it home and sealed the mouth with **black wax** (the wax seal on top of the vase in Part 1).
* Eli was never found. The vase's **painted face** (the cracked porcelain face of the demon) was painted to look like Eli.

## Rules of the thing (never break these)
1. It **never blinks**. Anything it possesses or imitates never blinks (Mom at dinner, Part 1).
2. It **copies the voices** of people you love to get *invited in* (Mom calling from downstairs, Part 1; Dad, Mom, you, and Eli in Part 2).
3. It **needs a home**. Broken out of the vase, it moved into the family's house and the family ("You broke my home. Now I live in yours.").
4. It **can't enter a home uninvited** — it knocks (Part 2: three slow knocks, "let me in").
5. It can only be sealed again if the vase is rebuilt **whole**, by the one who broke it, with **the face piece**, and it is **called home**.
6. Its true face is **cracked white porcelain with blue painting**; in the dark you see only **two red pinpoints**.

## Part 2 (VIDEO_014) facts
Pillowcase of pieces, rain, three streets; it follows on the roofs and calls in Dad's, Mom's and your own voice. Grandma makes you
**blink** before opening. She lays out the pieces: **23 of 24 — the face piece is missing** (still at the old house). She tells the 1968
story. Three slow knocks: a 12-year-old voice — "Rosie... it's Eli. Let me in." Eli would be 70 now. The peephole: a small porcelain face.
