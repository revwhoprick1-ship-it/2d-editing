# VIDEO_001 — Critical Review of Draft v1

*Written as a separate screenwriter and a bored viewer with a thumb on the scroll button. Draft v1 is not defended here.*

| Question | Verdict on v1 |
|---|---|
| Is the opening interesting? | **No.** "Imagine you were shrunk…" is the most generic "what if" opener there is. It has no stakes, no image and no question. |
| Does scene 1 communicate the premise? | Partly. "Shrunk" is clear, but nothing says *why I should care*. |
| Compelling reason to keep watching? | **No.** Danger only arrives at line 5 (about 60% in), and many viewers will already be gone. |
| Boring sections? | Lines 2–4 are a fact list with numbers ("7 to 8 micrometers", "5 liters", "half a micrometer") and nothing to feel. |
| Predictable discoveries? | Yes: blood, then heart, then lungs, in textbook order, with no surprise. |
| Feels like a biology list? | **Yes.** The mascot does nothing. He is carried. |
| Mascot active? | No. Every verb is passive ("you'd land", "carries you"). |
| Dangers understandable? | The neutrophil "attacks", but we never see why, how, or what it wants. |
| Enough escalation? | No. The heart is a fact, not a threat. Escalation is flat until a sudden attack. |
| Every scene new? | Mostly, but three scenes in a row work the same way (location + number). |
| Memorable ending? | **No.** "The body is amazing. Follow for more!" is filler and a cliché, and it pays off nothing. |
| Payoff worth waiting for? | No. "You manage to escape" is a cheat (deus ex machina). |
| Removable scenes? | Line 7 adds nothing. Line 3's "5 liters a minute" can't be pictured. |
| A more surprising twist? | **Yes, and it's missed:** it's *your own* body attacking *you*. That irony belongs in the first line, not line 5. |
| Visual humour? | None. |
| Stronger emotions? | Fear, awe and relief are all possible. v1 uses none. |
| Natural transitions? | No. "Then on to your lungs" teleports. The heart's own pumping should *be* the transition. |
| Accuracy | "Most common white blood cell" is fine. "Membrane just half a micrometer thick" is true but too abstract. "Pumps more than five liters every minute" is sourced (≈5.25 L/min) but adds nothing to the story. |

## Problems → Fixes for v2
1. **Move the threat into line 1.** Premise and conflict together: *"…and your own body is about to attack you."* This opens a specific information gap.
2. **Show scale through action, not numbers.** "That cushion you're riding? One red blood cell." The mascot pats it.
3. **Make the heart a threat and a transition.** Heartbeat audio grows, valves slam shut behind him, and "one squeeze fires you into your lungs".
4. **Replace abstract numbers with comparisons:** "a wall over a hundred times thinner than paper".
5. **Give the predator a reveal and a beat:** "Then… something notices you." Silence, then a reveal.
6. **Explain the irony in one line:** "It doesn't know you're you."
7. **Earn the escape:** he kicks free (active) and loses his shoe (cost and humour).
8. **Make the climax spectacular and grounded in fact:** the left ventricle (the main systemic pump) launches him into the aorta.
9. **End on a callback joke, not a CTA:** "…a white blood cell is still trying to digest your shoe." This rewards viewers who noticed the shoe.
10. **Remove** "follow for more", the 5‑liter line and the redundant "pumps you out into the rest of your body" line.
